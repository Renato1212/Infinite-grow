## Why this matters

Three separate readings, kept separate on purpose.

**Google Trends is an attention gauge, not a news source.** What the public is
searching tells you where crowded, reactive money is looking. Spikes precede
range expansion more often than they precede direction.

**Morning Bid gives you the cumulative story since the prior close** — the
narrative other participants walked in carrying. **The terminal gives you
isolated headlines**, which is a different thing: one is sentiment, one is event.

Keeping them in separate rows is what lets you ask, six months from now, whether
attention spikes actually predicted anything, without your answer being polluted
by the headline feed.

**Feeds:** Context.
